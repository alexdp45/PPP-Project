# Second Session with the Agent: November 4–5, 2026

A condensed record of how the question moved from off-market real estate to the GP–LP principal–agent relationship. My messages are lightly trimmed. The agent's responses are summarized. No names or personal details.

---

## Round 1: Start from Chapter 10

**Me:** The project needs to start from a framework in Ch. 10, such as the principal–agent relationship, and work back to a research question from my industry.

**Agent:** Read Ch. 10 ("Information: Contracts, Norms, and Power") and mapped eight of its frameworks to real estate:
- owner/manager principal–agent (Table 10.1)
- seller/listing broker
- the lemons problem
- standardization (the "hard red winter wheat #2" story)
- contract completeness and market structure (Table 10.4)
- how contract completeness divides the gains (Fig. 10.13)
- short-side power
- norms and trust

It also flagged that in this edition of the textbook, the credit model is Ch. 12, not Ch. 13.

## Round 2: GP–LP

**Me:** How about a GP–LP principal–agent relationship?

**Agent:** Checked it against Ch. 10's two conditions (conflict of interest, incomplete contract) and mapped the fund terms onto the model:
- promote = profit sharing
- re-upping = contingent renewal
- GP co-investment = collateral
- slow detection of bad deals = low *t*

It proposed comparing blind-pool funds with deal-by-deal sponsors.

## Round 3: Broaden beyond my niche

**Me:**
> It doesn't need to be IOS specific, simply exploring the GP–LP relationship in general. Give me different research questions that I can provably explore and come to a conclusion on.

**Agent:** Offered six questions, each with data collection and analysis options:
- does contingent renewal discipline GPs
- interim valuation inflation before fundraising
- fees vs. carry
- a waterfall risk-taking model
- continuation vehicles as a lemons problem
- short-side power in fundraising

## Round 4: My focus

**Me:**
> I have a broad network of both GPs and LPs I can interview as well. I'm curious on the GP incentives to deploy for fees rather than pick the best deals mostly.

**Agent:** Built a "deployment wedge" mechanism: fees, successor-fund clauses, and carry enter the GP's incentive compatibility constraint, creating a range of deals the GP wants and the LP doesn't. It also noted a second layer: deal teams paid on volume. It proposed a model, public data tests, and a structured interview protocol.

## Round 5: Scale

**Me:**
> I wonder if there's an attribute of returns to scale as well. At what scale does a syndicator or GP transition from focusing on being the best operator and securing outsized promote, vs simply allocating capital for fee based returns?

**Agent:** Pushed back that returns falling at scale isn't obvious, and that the evidence points both ways. It then showed that, since both fees and promote grow with fund size, a crossover needs either fixed platform costs or returns that shrink with size. That gives two measurable thresholds: the crossover fund size, and the wedge between LP-optimal and GP-optimal fund size.

## Round 6: Keep it principal–agent

**Me:**
> Remember the question needs to be based around the principal agent relationship between a GP and LP.

**Agent:** Reframed scale as the variable that changes the GP–LP relationship. As GPs grow, each of the LP's tools may weaken:
- profit sharing, because fees outgrow promote
- contingent renewal, because large GPs gain short-side power and LPs' threats stop being credible
- collateral, because GP co-investment shrinks as a share of the fund

This produced four sub-questions (incentive, behavior, outcome for LPs, LPs' response).

**My decision:** Go with this question. The README and CLAUDE.md were rewritten around it.
