# Initial Ideas

Ideas from my first email to the instructor and from the first brainstorming session, summarized rather than pasted. Personal details and names are removed.

## Starting thesis (first email)

- The most successful people and deals I've seen in real estate and business share one thing: an **asymmetric edge**, meaning information or capability the counterparty or competition doesn't have.
- **My own example: direct sourcing.** I don't wait for listings or broker calls. I've built a systematic process for finding, contacting, and building a relationship with property owners directly.
- **A second edge follows from the first.** Unrepresented owners usually know their operating business far better than real estate valuation. Buying from them works like rolling up small family-owned properties into an institutional-quality portfolio.
- **A parallel example in consumer lending.** A lender with a proprietary default-risk and pricing model can select and price loans better than competitors, then pool and securitize them with better performance.
- **General claim:** proprietary information about valuation, underwriting, or risk is what earns returns above what an efficient market allows. Value-add real estate depends on knowing what a property will be worth once your thesis is applied.
- **Problem I identified myself:** every idea I have comes back to "having an edge," which is too broad. I need to narrow it.

## Instructor feedback (paraphrased)

- If an edge persists, it's odd that competitors haven't competed it away. That's worth explaining, and in economics it resembles a technology.
- Identify the **actors**, their **actions**, and the **outcome** to be explained.
- The textbook's **Ch. 13 credit model** (credit, equity, collateral) could be a starting point, even though it's stylized.

## Refinements from the brainstorming session

- **Off-market doesn't automatically mean a discount.** It often produces a better deal, but not always.
- **Off-market deals are costly.** Sourcing takes a lot of time, and without a broker to mediate, negotiating with owners can be harder, depending on who owns the property.
- **I'd usually prefer a broker involved,** but brokers tend to push prices to unreasonable levels.
- **Most of my sellers have been older, retiring owners.** Timing ("right place, right time") matters a lot.
- **Brokers often take listings they know won't sell at the asking price.** That clutters the market and wastes everyone's time.
- **Owners today are often unrealistic about value.** They chase the highest price on paper over certainty of closing.
- **Institutional capital arrived in IOS in force during COVID.** These buyers have a very low cost of capital and are often paid for deploying capital at scale rather than on performance.
- **The main idea now:** institutional capital has crowded smaller operators out of the listed market. That pushes them into the less efficient off-market channel, where they compete for edge through search and relationships instead of cheap capital.
- **I don't want the project to be only about my own methods or my frustrations** with institutional buyers. It should explain how the market works.
